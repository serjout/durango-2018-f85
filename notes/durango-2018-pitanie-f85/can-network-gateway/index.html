    2018 Dodge Durango (WD) 5.7L AWD — CAN network, gateway, star connectors: common POWER failure vs common COMMUNICATION failure¶

    Q1. Which modules are on CAN-C and which on CAN-IHS (and on any private buses)? Covers ORC, BSM, HVAC, radio, ParkSense, camera, IPC, BCM, RF Hub, PCM.¶

    Takeaway¶

    The 2018 Durango has two CAN-C star connectors and two CAN-IHS star connectors, each pair joined by a trunk: - CAN-C: "Dash" star and "IP" star, joined by D64/D65. - CAN-IHS: "IP" star and "Body" star, joined by D145/D146.

    The modules that matter here are on different buses and different stars: - ORC: CAN-C (IP star). - BSM radars: CAN-IHS (Body star). - A/C heater (HVAC) module: CAN-IHS (IP star). - Park Assist module: CAN-C (Dash star). - Radio: not on either star. It has point-to-point "A/T" CAN-C and CAN-IHS links to the Security Gateway. - Rear camera: no CAN pins at all. Its only network wire is BCM LIN "CBC 3".

    Inferences¶

    • ORC (CAN-C, IP star C2) and the BSM sensors (CAN-IHS, Body star C4/C5) share no bus segment. The A/C heater module is on a third star (IHS-IP C3). The blower and the camera are not CAN loads at all.
    • A single CAN fault therefore cannot take out exactly these four functions while sparing the modules that share their stars:
    • the IPC, RF Hub and steering column module (IP star, with ORC);
    • the door modules (IHS-IP star, with the A/C heater module);
    • the liftgate and seat modules (IHS-Body star, with the BSM sensors).
    • The radio shows the camera screen on reverse. Reverse status comes from the shift-by-wire/TCM side on CAN-C and reaches the radio through the SGW "A/T" link. So the path CAN-C → SGW → radio is working.

    Gaps¶

    • End views for the branch plugs C2–C13 were not fetched. Their pin numbers are inferred from the diagram, and pin 1 = (+) is assumed by analogy with the C1 end views. The CAN C IP C1 end view (D2599A) was also not fetched.
    • BCM pin numbers for its CAN and LIN connections were not in the extracted text.
    • The rear camera's power and video circuits are on the Radio/Navigation diagrams, which were not fetched.
    • Whether this R/T actually has each optional module (ACC, active damping, video) depends on the build, not on the diagram.

    Q2. Does the BCM act as the gateway? Does the 2018 Durango (VIN digit 10 = J) have a Security Gateway (SGW)?¶

    Takeaway¶

    Both are true: - The BCM is the central gateway between CAN-C and CAN-IHS. - A Security Gateway Module (SGW, also written SGM) is part of the 2018 Durango service information and wiring. It sits between the diagnostic port (DLC) and Uconnect radio on one side and the internal CAN-C and CAN-IHS on the other.

    Cited Findings¶

    • VERIFIED quote: the architecture "allows the Body Control Module (BCM) to be a Central Gateway to process and transfer messages" between CAN C and CAN IHS. Another VERIFIED quote: "These networks and their subnetworks are protected by a Security Gateway Module (SGM)." — SM-CommOp
    • VERIFIED: the 2018 Durango manual has a separate SGW DTC list (all three were read):
    • B2199-14/-16/-17 battery voltage;
    • B22A9-49 internal failure;
    • B235A-00 in-plant mode;
    • C221C-00 flash required;
    • U0001-00 CAN C BUS;
    • U0002-00 CAN C BUS OFF PERFORMANCE;
    • U0011-00 CAN INTERIOR BUS OFF PERFORMANCE.

    Sources: SGW DTC index, SGW Electrical Diagnostics - VERIFIED: the manual also has an SGW removal procedure and SGW connector end views C1 (D3921A) and C2 (D3921B). Neither was fetched — SGW U0011 test (links), StarList - SGW wiring on data-lines sheet 1. The diagram shows no "with SGW" option note. Location text: "RIGHT CENTER OF DASH". STRONG INFERENCE pin map — CDL-1: - SGW C1: - pin 3 D428 TAN "DIA CAN C (+)" - pin 4 D436 "CAN C (+) AT" (to radio) - pin 5 D351 "CAN C (+)" (to CAN-C IP star C6) - pin 6 Z912 GND - pin 7 F949 "FSD IGN RUN/START" - pin 10 D427 YEL "DIA CAN C (−)" - pin 11 D435 "CAN C (−) AT" - pin 12 D350 "CAN C (−)" - SGW C2: - pin 1 D437 "CAN IHS (+) AT" - pin 2 D434 "DIA CAN IHS (+)" - pin 3 D353 "CAN IHS (+)" (to IHS-IP star C6) - pin 4 Z912 - pin 5 A902 "FSD B(+)" - pin 6 D438 "CAN IHS (−) AT" - pin 7 D433 "DIA CAN IHS (−)" - pin 8 D352 "CAN IHS (−)" - DLC ("UNDER LEFT END OF DASH"): - pins 6/14 = D428/D427 (diagnostic CAN C, to the SGW only); - pins 3/11 = D434/D433 (diagnostic CAN IHS, to the SGW only); - pins 4/5 = Z911 (ground G911A); - pin 16 = A900 B+. - SGW supply: - PDC Fuse 44 (10 A) and Fuse 45 (5 A), "hot at all times", feed DLC pin 16 and SGW B+. Which fuse feeds which was not resolved. - Fuse 88 (15 A), "hot w/ run/start relay energized", feeds splice S2450 and then SGW F949. - Trade press on fleet rollout (not Durango-specific): - Mopar's media manager said the SGW "was placed on most 2018 model year and newer FCA vehicles". The same article quotes CIC's Rozint saying RAM and Jeep Grand Cherokee received SGWs in 2019 — Repairer Driven News, 2020-01-20 - IOSiX said FCA put the SGW in "roughly half of their 2018 product line and nearly 90 percent of their 2019 vehicles" — VehicleServicePros, 2018-10-09 - Aftermarket bypass sellers list the 2018–2023 Durango as SGW-equipped. These are commercial sources — garageandfab, buyp3 - What the SGW blocks: the FCA overview says the SGW "does not restrict access to diagnostic data". Clearing DTCs and bi-directional tests need an authenticated tool (AutoAuth) — Repairer Driven News

    Inferences¶

    • Three different gateway behaviours follow from this layout:
    • The DLC and the radio talk to the vehicle only through the SGW, over separate "DIA" and "A/T" links.
    • The SGW itself is an ordinary node on CAN-C (IP star C6) and on CAN-IHS (IHS-IP star C6).
    • The BCM bridges messages between CAN-C (Dash star C7) and CAN-IHS (trunk D145/D146).
    • What an SGW failure would look like: the scan tool would see nothing on either bus, and the radio would lose all vehicle data, including reverse status and HVAC status. The user's radio still detects reverse, so the SGW and its F949 ignition feed are alive. STRONG INFERENCE.
    • Workshop practice: an aftermarket scan tool will read codes but needs AutoAuth (or a 12+8 bypass harness) to clear them. This matters when clearing ORC or BSM codes after the repair.

    Gaps¶

    • The service-information text gives no physical SGW location beyond the diagram note "right center of dash". The removal procedure was not read.
    • I did not find an FCA document that ties SGW fitment to a production date or VIN range for the Durango. The evidence is the 2018 WD service information plus vendor lists.

    Q3. Where are the CAN star connectors, what are their pinouts, and how does a technician isolate a branch (resistance, voltage)?¶

    Takeaway¶

    • Construction: each "star" is a housing that takes separate small plugs, one per branch (C1…C13). C1 is the 3-pin trunk: CAN(+), CAN(−) and a ground. Every other plug is a 2-pin module branch.
    • Termination: the termination resistors are inside the star connectors. Total bus resistance is about 60 Ω with the battery disconnected.
    • Isolation: unplug one branch plug at the star to separate that module and its wiring from the bus.

    Cited Findings¶

    • VERIFIED: "Termination resistors are built into the Star Connectors." Total network resistance is about 60 Ω, measured with the battery disconnected. The bus is a twisted pair with 33–50 twists per metre — SM-CommOp
    • VERIFIED voltage table (DVOM between node ground and the CAN terminal; the meter shows an average) — SM-CommOp:
    Condition CAN-L CAN-H
    Asleep / key off 0 V 0 V
    CAN-C recessive (idle) 2.4–2.5 V 2.4–2.5 V
    Active (dominant / key on) 1.3–2.3 V 2.6–3.5 V
    Short to ground ≈0 V ≈0 V (0.02 V)
    Short to battery battery voltage battery voltage − 0.75 V
    CAN-H shorted to CAN-L 2.45 V 2.45 V
    • Star connector C1 end views (VERIFIED, from OEM connector pages):
    • D2600A, STAR CAN C DASH C1 (natural colour, female, part 2-2138651-1): pin 1 D65 WH/GN CAN C (+); pin 2 D64 WH/BU CAN C (−); pin 3 Z909 BK ground — D2600A
    • D2601A, STAR CAN IHS IP C1 (GREEN, female, part 5-2138651-1): pin 1 D145 WH/GN CAN IHS (+); pin 2 D146 WH CAN IHS (−); pin 3 Z912 BK ground — D2601A
    • D2602A, STAR CAN IHS BODY C1 (natural colour, part 7283-8349): pin 1 D145 WH/GN CAN IHS (+); pin 2 D146 WH CAN IHS (−) — D2602A
    • VERIFIED: complete list of star plugs in the OEM connector index — StarList:
    • CAN C Dash: C1, C2, C3, C5–C12 (D2600A–M)
    • CAN C IP: C1–C7, C10, C12 (D2599A–L)
    • CAN IHS IP: C1, C2, C3, C5, C6, C7, C10–C13 (D2601A–M)
    • CAN IHS Body: C1–C6, C11, C12 (D2602A–L)
    • Branch plugs for the modules in question (STRONG INFERENCE, see Q1). Plug pin 1 = (+), pin 2 = (−):
    • ORC = CAN-C IP star C2 (D11/D12)
    • IPC = CAN-C IP C5 (D81/D82)
    • RF Hub = CAN-C IP C12 (D39/D40)
    • SGW = CAN-C IP C6 and IHS-IP C6
    • BCM = CAN-C Dash C7 (D45/D46)
    • PCM = CAN-C Dash C2
    • Park Assist = CAN-C Dash C8 (D77/D78)
    • A/C heater = IHS-IP C3 (D101/D102)
    • Left BSM = IHS-Body C4 (D215/D216)
    • Right BSM = IHS-Body C5 (D217/D218)

Check the scan-tool topology ("Vehicle View"). 2. Load-test the fused B+ and ignition supplies with a 3156 bulb (about 2 A). Check the fuse first; if it is open, look for a short to ground. The procedure warns: "Do not load test air bag/restraint circuits." 3. Load-test the grounds. 4. Measure CAN (+) at the ECU connector. Below 2.6 V means an open or high resistance in CAN (+). 5. Measure CAN (−). Below 1.3 V means an open in CAN (−). 6. Inspect the connectors, then replace the ECU if it is still inactive. - VERIFIED: the SGW U0001/U0011 tests say to "use the wiring diagrams and CAN C/IHS star connectors to isolate opens, shorts, or a faulty module". The "Scan Tool Will Not Communicate With CAN Bus" procedure "diagnoses CAN Bus circuits after the Security Gateway". It also warns that U-codes may not appear until the fault is repaired — SGW U0011 test, Scan Tool Will Not Communicate With CAN Bus

    Inferences¶

    • Isolation method (standard star-topology practice, consistent with the OEM facts above):
    • Disconnect the battery and measure across CAN(+) and CAN(−) at any branch. About 60 Ω means two good terminations in parallel.
    • If one bus is down, unplug branch plugs at the star one at a time and watch the bus voltage and resistance recover. The branch that restores the bus is the shorted one.
    • With a branch plug pulled, the module-side pins should not show a low resistance across H–L or to ground.
    • Per-star values: UNVERIFIED. Because termination is inside the stars, a separated star (trunk C1 unplugged) probably reads about 120 Ω on its own. The manual does not give per-star values.
    • Single-module case (ORC, BSM): the OEM "No Response From ECU" flow checks power and ground before CAN. A dead fuse feed (F85 at 0 V on both sides) puts the ORC fault in steps 2–3, not in CAN.
    • Shared BSM hardware: both BSM sensors' CAN pairs run through the same in-line connectors, I701 and XY4300A (rear-bumper harness). A fault at those connectors could drop both BSM sensors together without any star or bus fault. Their power feed probably passes there too; that is UNVERIFIED and should be checked on the BSM diagram.

    Gaps¶

    • The CAN C IP star end views and all C2…C13 branch end views were not fetched.
    • The written OEM location descriptions (as opposed to figures) and the CAN C IP star location figure were not obtained.
    • The "Scan Tool Will Not Communicate With Vehicle" and "Stored Lost Communication DTCs" diagnostic tests were not fetched. They may contain explicit resistance-per-branch steps.

    Q4. Could one CAN-IHS branch failure cause SRS lamp + BSM + blower + camera failures while the engine runs? Which symptoms do NOT fit a pure CAN failure? Does the HVAC module drive the blower directly (hard-wired) or over CAN?¶

    Takeaway¶

    No. These symptoms are not consistent with a single CAN failure of any kind. The four functions sit on CAN-C, CAN-IHS (Body star), a hard-wired high-current circuit, and LIN plus video respectively. Modules sharing each of those stars still work.

    The front blower is driven by wire: - The A/C heater module sends a hard-wired "BLOWER CTRL" signal (circuit C56) to the blower power module. - The power comes from the PDC "Blower Motor Relay" and Fuse 13 (40 A).

    So "UI works, speed bars change, motor dead" is a power-path symptom.

    Cited Findings¶

    • Front blower chain (STRONG INFERENCE from the A/C circuit SVG text) — AC-1, AC-2:
    • PDC side: a "BLOWER MOTOR RELAY" with "FUSE 13" 40 A. The rear blower relay has Fuse 37, 25 A.
    • Supply: relay output circuit C7 (DK BLU) → XY5020A pin 29 → front blower motor power module C1-6 "BLOWER MTR SPLY" → module C2-1 C7 (DK BLU/WHT) → blower motor pin 1.
    • Motor return: motor pin 2 "BLOWER MTR CTRL" (Z134, DK BLU/BRN) → power module C2-2 → module ground C1-5 Z911 → G911A (under front of center console).
    • Control: A/C heater module circuit C56 (DK BLU/BLU) → power module C1-3 "BLOWER CTRL". The label printed beside the C56 pin on the A/C module reads "BLOWER MOTOR SPLY".
    • No network at the power module: WebFetch reported no "CAN", "LIN" or "PWM" text on the power-module sheet.
    • STRONG INFERENCE: the A/C heater module's own network connection is CAN IHS on C1 pins 4/5 (D101/D102 → IHS-IP star C3) — AC-1, CDL-2
    • Modules sharing stars with the failed functions (STRONG INFERENCE):
    • CAN-C IP star: ORC (C2) shares it with the IPC (C5), steering column module (C7), RF Hub (C12) and SGW (C6) — CDL-3
    • CAN-IHS Body star: the BSM sensors (C4/C5) share it with the power liftgate module (C2) and the seat modules — CDL-5
    • IHS-IP star: the A/C module (C3) shares it with the driver and passenger door modules (C2/C10) — CDL-2
    • STRONG INFERENCE: the rear camera's only network connection is BCM LIN CBC 3. It has no CAN pins — CDL-4
    • VERIFIED: the OEM ECU no-response flow load-tests power and ground before checking CAN (see Q3) — No Response From ECU

    Inferences¶

    • A full CAN-C failure is ruled out. It would take down the IPC, RF Hub, steering column module, PCM/TCM/ABS messaging and SGW→radio reverse status. Keyless start, a working cluster and "radio detects reverse" contradict that.
    • A full CAN-IHS failure is ruled out. It would break:
    • the door-module functions on the IHS-IP star: passenger window from the driver switch, door locks, mirrors;
    • fob-commanded liftgate on the IHS-Body star.

    All of these work. Caveat: if the liftgate was tested only with its own hard-wired switch, that does not prove CAN-IHS. - One IHS branch (one 2-pin plug) can drop one module only. Both BSM sensors dropping together fits a shared rear-bumper in-line connector (I701/XY4300A) or a shared power feed better than a bus fault. - Symptoms that do NOT fit any CAN failure: - Front blower motor dead while the UI responds. The motor is powered through Fuse 13 (40 A), the blower relay, circuit C7 and the power module. A CAN loss would leave the module unable to command, but the motor path itself is hard-wired. - Rear camera blue screen. The camera is not a CAN node. The radio switching to the camera view proves the CAN/A-T reverse message arrives. A blue (no-video) screen points to camera power, ground, the video circuit or the camera itself. - ORC dark with F85 at 0 V on both sides. This is a power problem upstream of F85 (the feed to the fuse), not a CAN problem. - Best fit: a common power-source failure that feeds the ORC fuse F85, the blower relay or power module supply, the BSM sensor supply and the camera supply. Engine, charging and CAN infrastructure are intact. - Hypothesis for the power-distribution researcher (UNVERIFIED): the A/C sheet prints "HOT W/ RUN 2 RELAY ENERGIZED" near the blower relays. If F85, the blower relay coil feed and the BSM/camera fuses all hang off one PDC ignition-run relay output or bus, that relay or its feed is the prime suspect. This needs confirmation from the PDC / power-distribution diagrams.

    Gaps¶

    • Which PDC relay or bus feeds the blower motor relay coil (and F85) was not resolved from the SVG text.
    • Whether the Uconnect speed bars show the A/C module's echoed status or only the radio's own command was not documented. So the UI working does not by itself prove the A/C heater module is online. A topology scan is needed.
    • The power feeds of the BSM sensors and the rear camera were not extracted. They are not on the data-lines sheets.

    Q5. Which modules need ORC messages? Do the BSM radars or the camera depend on the ORC?¶

    Takeaway¶

    No source I could reach shows the BSM sensors, the rear camera or the HVAC/blower depending on ORC messages. On the network the ORC is a CAN-C node (IP star C2), electrically independent of the CAN-IHS Body star where the BSM sensors sit. An ORC outage should light the SRS lamp, nothing more.

    Cited Findings¶

    • STRONG INFERENCE: ORC network pins are C2 pin 54 "CAN C (+)" (D11) and pin 53 "CAN C (−)" (D12), via XY5020A 11/12 to CAN-C IP star C2 — CDL-3
    • STRONG INFERENCE: the BSM sensors connect only to CAN-IHS Body star plugs C4/C5. The rear camera connects only to BCM LIN CBC 3 — CDL-5, CDL-4
    • VERIFIED: the OEM "No Response From ECU" procedure explicitly excludes load-testing air-bag/restraint circuits. ORC power checks must be done by voltage or approved method only — No Response From ECU

    Inferences¶

    • Expected DTC pattern with the ORC unpowered: the cluster and other CAN-C nodes log "lost communication with ORC" (generic U0151) and the cluster lights the airbag lamp. Typical FCA behaviour; not confirmed in a 2018 Durango document.
    • BSM, camera and blower: with no documented ORC dependency, their simultaneous failure points to a shared power source, not to the ORC's silence.
    • Scan-tool check that settles it: a topology scan showing ORC, left and right BSM sensors "not responding", while the A/C heater module, BCM, door modules and liftgate respond, confirms a power-feed problem. The next step is voltage checks at each module's fused B+/ignition pins, not a CAN hunt.

    Gaps¶

    • I found no 2018 Durango description of which modules consume ORC broadcast messages (crash status, belt status). The BCM U-code list (DTCs B2119-11 to U1433-24) and the IPC DTC pages were not read.
    • The BSM system's own "Description and Operation" was not fetched; it might list required input messages such as vehicle speed, turn signals or trailer status.
